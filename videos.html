See past the fear.

Fear killed Shoreham once — fear built on myths about waste, radiation, and meltdowns that don't survive contact with the evidence. These videos, from physicists, engineers, and independent journalists, take on the biggest anti-nuclear claims one by one. Watch them, share them, and send them to the skeptic in your group chat.

The Claim: "Nuclear power is a lie — it's dirty, dangerous, and dying"

Start here: the big picture

The reality: nuclear is among the safest, cleanest energy sources ever measured per unit of electricity produced. These two explain what really stalled it in America — and it wasn't the science.

The Claim: "There's no solution for nuclear waste"

The waste myth

The reality: all U.S. spent commercial fuel ever produced would fit on a single football field stacked about 10 yards high, sealed in engineered casks with a flawless storage safety record. It's the most carefully managed waste stream of any industry.

Kyle Hill

I Kissed Nuclear Waste to Prove a Point

Science educator Kyle Hill visits a working nuclear plant and puts his lips on a dry storage cask holding spent fuel — because the physics says he can. Over a million views of pure myth demolition.

Use it for: the "glowing green goop" mental image. Real waste is solid ceramic pellets in steel and concrete.

The Claim: "We stopped building nuclear because it's too dangerous"

What actually happened to American nuclear

The reality: fear after Three Mile Island — an accident with no detectable public health impact — plus shifting regulation and financing costs froze the industry. This is Shoreham's own story, told nationally.
